A Simple Monthly Budget That Actually Works

A budget does not need an app or a spreadsheet degree. One page, five steps, and a little saved every week — that is the whole system.

Step 1: Write down what comes in

Start with the easy number: what actually lands in your account each month, after tax. If your pay changes month to month, use the lowest month from the last three — a budget built on your best month will break in a bad one. Write the number at the top of your monthly budget planner. That is the money you have to work with, and everything below has to fit inside it.

Step 2: List the bills that never move

Next, write down the fixed bills: rent or mortgage, power, water, phone, internet, insurance, car payment. These are the same every month, so just write them down with their due dates and add them up. What is left after the fixed bills is your real spending money, and for most people it is smaller than they expect.

Step 3: Give every dollar a job

Now split what is left into buckets: groceries, gas, fun, clothes, and savings. Give every dollar a job before the month starts, so there is no “leftover” money drifting away on things you cannot remember. The rule is simple: income minus everything you plan to spend should equal zero. That does not mean you spend it all — savings is one of the buckets, and it gets its share first.

Step 4: Track what you actually spend

A plan is only useful if you check it. Each week, write what you actually spent next to what you planned — the budget planner has a planned-versus-actual column for exactly this. You will be wrong in the first month, and that is not failure, it is information. Adjust next month’s numbers and carry on.

Step 5: Save a little every week

Saving works better in small weekly amounts than in one big monthly promise. The 52-week savings challenge starts at $1 in week one, $2 in week two, and ends at $1,378 by week fifty-two — one checkbox per week, one page. Even if you only do the first half of the year, that is still hundreds of dollars you would not have saved otherwise. Put the sheet on the fridge where you will see it, and check the box every week.

That is the whole system: know what comes in, list what never moves, give every dollar a job, check what you actually spent, and save a little every week.
